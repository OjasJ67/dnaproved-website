Bird DNA sexing

Male or female, read from the bird's own DNA in 2 business days.

$18per bird. First bird half price on your first order. Free kit with return postage.
Order bird sexing

How to collect a sample

  • Feathers: gently pluck a few small chest feathers. Use plucked feathers, not ones that fell out, because the DNA is in the root. Seal them in a clean paper envelope.
  • Swab: rub the swab firmly inside the beak for about 15 seconds, let it air dry, then cap it.
  • Eggshell membrane: keep the shell of a freshly hatched egg with its inner membrane. Don't wash it.
  • Blood card: best taken by your avian vet or an experienced keeper. Let the spot dry fully before posting.

Label every sample with the bird's band or microchip number. Your kit includes envelopes, labels and return postage.

What you get back

A certificate naming the result (DNAproved male or DNAproved female), the bird's identification number and the test date, with a QR code that opens the photo of the gel your bird ran on. Breeders can share it with buyers as proof.

Which birds

Most captive-bred parrots, pigeons, doves, finches, canaries, softbills and domestic poultry. A few groups need different primers, and ostriches and emus may not be covered, so ask us first. We can't accept feathers of native North American wild birds; federal law protects them.

Every run includes a known male, a known female, an extraction blank and a no-template control. We keep your bird's DNA for 90 days, so any re-test is free.

DNA species ID

What your specimen is, matched by its DNA barcode, with the sequence to show for it.

$40per specimen. 10% off 5 or more, 15% off 20 or more.
Order species ID

What we identify

  • Isopods and other invertebrates, including hobby morphs sold under trade names.
  • Insects, from pest checks to collection specimens.
  • Fish samples, such as a fillet you want to check against its label.
  • Mushroom cultures and dried fungi, for growers and mycology clubs.

How to send

Send dead, dried or frozen specimens, never live animals. One or two whole adults is plenty for most invertebrates. For fungi, send a small piece of dried tissue or a sealed culture. Tell us the name it was sold or labelled under.

What you get back

A dated, signed report with the best reference match, its percent identity, the barcode gene we used (COI for animals, ITS for fungi) and a QR link to the sequence trace. Sellers can add a public record page and a DNAproved species badge for their listings.

Many hobby isopods are undescribed species sold under trade names, so public databases may have nothing to match. We're building a reference library from respected breeders' founder stock, and your report says plainly when no confident match exists.

eDNA water screening

Find out whether a target species lives in your water, from a filter rather than a net.

$135per sample, 1 to 3 target species. No ten-sample minimum.
Ask about eDNA

Who it's for

Lake associations, towns, land trusts, environmental consultants and pond owners who need to know if an invasive species has arrived, or if a rare one is still there.

How it works

  • We send a field kit, or help you sample anywhere in New Jersey.
  • You filter a measured volume of water and keep the filter cold.
  • We extract the DNA and run species-specific PCR for each target, alongside field blanks and lab blanks.

What you get back

Detected or not detected for each target species, with the amplification data and the controls behind it. Ask which target species we've validated.

eDNA results are screening information, not regulatory findings. A detection shows the species' DNA was present, not how many animals live there. Before sampling, we agree with you who will report a positive invasive-species result to state agencies.

Sample acceptance policy.

These rules keep every test legal and safe. We return or destroy anything outside them, untested.

  • No human samples of any kind. We are not a clinical lab and don't do paternity, ancestry or medical tests.
  • Captive-bred and domestic birds only. Federal law protects the feathers of native North American wild birds, so we can't accept them.
  • No live animals. Many isopods and other invertebrates need a USDA permit to keep, so send specimens dead, dried or frozen.
  • No disease diagnosis. We work only with low-risk organisms at biosafety level 1. For a sick animal, see your vet.
  • Screening, not certification. eDNA and seafood results are screening information, not regulatory findings.
  • Sourdough starter stays local. It's sold in person within New Jersey only, under a cottage food permit.

Questions people ask.

Can't find yours? Ask the assistant in the corner or email dna.proved@gmail.com.

How do I know the result is right?

Every run includes a known male and a known female bird, an extraction blank and a no-template PCR control, and a run with a failed control is repeated, never reported. We validate each test on samples of known identity before we sell it, send blind duplicates to another lab each quarter, and link every report to its raw gel photo or sequence trace so you can see the evidence yourself.

Which birds can you sex?

Most captive-bred parrots, pigeons, doves, finches, canaries and domestic poultry. A few groups need different primers and ratites such as ostriches may not be covered, so ask before sending.

Can you sex a chick from its eggshell?

Yes. Keep the shell of a freshly hatched egg with its inner membrane, don't wash it, and send it in a paper envelope labelled with the chick's ID.

How fast will I get results?

Bird sexing results come back 2 business days after your sample arrives. Sequencing-based tests take a little longer, and we confirm timing when you order.

How do I pay?

Nothing is charged on this website. After you send a request, we email you the kit, the final price and how to pay.

What if my isopod isn't in any database?

Then your report says so, and shows the closest match with its percent identity. Many hobby isopods are undescribed species, which is why we're building a reference library of trade names from respected breeders' founder stock.

Will a positive eDNA result be reported to the state?

We agree that with you before sampling. eDNA results are screening information, and we settle in advance who reports a positive invasive-species detection to the agencies.

Can you ship sourdough starter?

No. Under New Jersey's cottage food rules, starter is sold in person within the state only.

How long do you keep my sample?

We keep extracted DNA for 90 days after your result, so a re-test in that time is free.

Do you test human DNA?

No. We never accept human samples. DNAProved tests animal, environmental and microbial samples only.

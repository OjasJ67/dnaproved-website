Read a gel yourself.

This is how a bird sexing result looks before it becomes a certificate. Tap any lane.

  • One band: both copies of the CHD1 gene are Z. The bird is male (ZZ).
  • Two bands: one Z copy and one shorter W copy. The bird is female (ZW).
  • Controls: a known male, a known female and an empty blank run beside every sample.

Looks can't tell you.

A breeder pairs two birds and waits a whole season for eggs that never come. A collector pays for a rare isopod and receives a common one. The eye is a poor judge of what DNA settles in days.

What to send, what you get.

Pick a test to see how to collect your sample, what comes back and what to know before you order.

How to collect

  • Feathers: gently pluck a few small chest feathers. Plucked, not fallen out, because the DNA is in the root. Seal them in a paper envelope.
  • Swab: rub inside the beak for about 15 seconds, air dry, cap it.
  • Eggshell membrane: keep the shell of a freshly hatched egg with its inner membrane. Don't wash it.
  • Blood card: best taken by your avian vet or an experienced keeper. Let it dry fully.

What you get back

A certificate reading DNAproved male or DNAproved female, with the bird's band or microchip number, the test date and a QR code to the photo of the gel your bird ran on.

Which birds

Most captive-bred parrots, pigeons, doves, finches, canaries, softbills and poultry. Ask first for ostriches and emus. We can't accept feathers of native North American wild birds.

$18

per bird, 2 business days. First bird half price on your first order. Free kit with return postage.

From feather to finished report.

The same six steps sit behind every result, and the last one hands you the evidence. Watch the helix unzip, the way it does at 95 °C in every PCR cycle.

  1. Request a kit

    Tell us what you need tested. We send a free collection kit with instructions and return postage, or you drop off in New Jersey by appointment.

  2. Collect and send

    Pluck a few feathers, fill a blood card, swab, filter water or pack a dead specimen. Each sample gets its own ID the day it arrives.

  3. We extract the DNA

    In a clean pre-PCR area we break open the cells and purify the DNA. Tubes, pipettes and gloves never travel back from the post-PCR area.

  4. PCR copies the target

    A thermocycler copies only the gene that answers your question, about a billion times in 30 cycles.

  5. We read the result

    Bird sexing is read on an agarose gel. Species and microbe IDs are Sanger sequenced and matched against reference databases. eDNA is read by species-specific PCR.

  6. Your report, with the evidence

    A signed report with a QR code that opens the raw gel photo or sequence trace. We keep your extracted DNA for 90 days, so re-tests are free.

What's in every run.

A run only counts if its controls pass. If one fails, we repeat the run. We never report it.

  • Known male and known female birds. They prove the test can tell the sexes apart in this run.
  • Extraction blank. A tube that goes through extraction with no sample. If DNA turns up, something was contaminated.
  • No-template control. PCR with water in place of DNA. Any band here and the run is repeated.
  • Outside checks. Each quarter, a few blind duplicates go to another lab to check our answers.

What we don't accept

  • Human samples of any kind. We are not a clinical lab.
  • Feathers of native North American wild birds. Captive-bred cage birds and poultry only.
  • Live invertebrates. Send dead, dried or frozen specimens.
  • Any sample sent to diagnose disease. We work only with low-risk organisms at biosafety level 1.
  • eDNA and seafood results are screening information, not regulatory findings.
  • Sourdough starter is sold in person within New Jersey only.

Questions people ask.

Can't find yours? Ask the assistant in the corner or email dna.proved@gmail.com.

How do I know the result is right?

Every run includes a known male and a known female bird, an extraction blank and a no-template PCR control, and a run with a failed control is repeated, never reported. We validate each test on samples of known identity before we sell it, send blind duplicates to another lab each quarter, and link every report to its raw gel photo or sequence trace.

Which birds can you sex?

Most captive-bred parrots, pigeons, doves, finches, canaries and domestic poultry. A few groups need different primers and ratites such as ostriches may not be covered, so ask before sending.

Can you sex a chick from its eggshell?

Yes. Keep the shell of a freshly hatched egg with its inner membrane, don't wash it, and send it in a paper envelope labelled with the chick's ID.

How fast will I get results?

Bird sexing results come back 2 business days after your sample arrives. Sequencing-based tests take a little longer, and we confirm timing when you order.

How do I pay?

Nothing is charged on this website. After you send a request, we email you the kit, the final price and how to pay.

What if my isopod isn't in any database?

Then your report says so, and shows the closest match with its percent identity. Many hobby isopods are undescribed species, which is why we're building a reference library of trade names from respected breeders' founder stock.

Will a positive eDNA result be reported to the state?

We agree that with you before sampling. eDNA results are screening information, and we settle in advance who reports a positive invasive-species detection.

Can you ship sourdough starter?

No. Under New Jersey's cottage food rules, starter is sold in person within the state only.

How long do you keep my sample?

We keep extracted DNA for 90 days after your result, so a re-test in that time is free.

Do you test human DNA?

No. We never accept human samples. DNAProved tests animal, environmental and microbial samples only.
